5FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers on dates, prices, routes, eligibility, visas and the yatra itself.

Dates & Booking

Is booking open for Kailash Mansarovar Yatra 2027?
Yes. 2027 departures (May–September) are open now, including Full-Moon batches. Seats are limited per batch by Chinese permit quotas, so we recommend reserving with the USD 300 deposit as early as possible.
When is the best time to do the Kailash yatra?
The yatra season runs from May to September — outside it, snow closes the high passes and the Tibet border routes. June and September usually have the most settled weather; July and August are the busiest months, so book 2–3 months ahead for those.
Why are Full-Moon departures special?
On Purnima night the reflection of the full moon on Lake Mansarovar is considered the most auspicious sight of the yatra. Our Full-Moon batches are timed so you are at the lake on that night — at no extra cost. They are the most in-demand dates and fill first.
When do the helicopter and Lhasa tours depart?
The 9-day Helicopter tour Ex. Lucknow and the 15-day Kailash Tour via Lhasa run May–September 2027 on dates arranged on request — small groups and private departures are both possible. WhatsApp us your preferred month and we'll send available dates.

Cost & Inclusions

What does the yatra cost and what is included?
Per person, twin sharing: Overland 14 Days for NRI / foreigners USD 2,300 · Overland 13 Days for Indian citizens ₹ 2,49,000 (₹ 2,59,000 in instalments) · Helicopter 9 Days Ex. Lucknow USD 3,080 · Via Lhasa 15 Days on request. Prices include accommodation, vegetarian meals, transport, all Tibet permits, visa processing, guides and oxygen — see "What's Included" below for the full list.
Are there any extra costs during the yatra?
Plan for international flights, travel insurance, the China visa fee for non-Indian passports (USD 260; USD 360 for US / Canada), an optional pony (approx. RMB 4,500–5,000) or porter (approx. RMB 1,300–1,500) on the Kora, and tips and personal expenses.
Can I pay in instalments? Are there group discounts?
Yes. Indian citizens can pay ₹ 2,59,000 in instalments instead of ₹ 2,49,000 in full; the NRI / foreign price of USD 2,300 is the same either way. We offer group, family and repeat-pilgrim discounts, and private groups of 5 or more can travel on their own dates.

Routes

What are the main routes to Kailash from Nepal?
There are three: Overland via Kerung (Gyirong) — by road from Kathmandu to the Kerung border, about 150 km north, then across the plateau via Saga to Mansarovar; Helicopter via Simikot – Hilsa — flights to Nepalgunj and Simikot, a helicopter to the Hilsa border, then a short drive from Taklakot (Purang); and via Lhasa — fly to Lhasa to acclimatise and see the Potala Palace, then travel west across Tibet.
Overland or helicopter — which should I choose?
The Overland tour from Kathmandu is our most popular — best value, gradual acclimatisation and Full-Moon dates. It takes 14 days for NRI / foreign passports and 13 days for Indian passports. The Helicopter tour (9 days, Ex. Lucknow) saves long drives by flying to Simikot and taking a helicopter to the Hilsa border. The Lhasa route adds Potala Palace and Tibetan culture. Try the "Help me choose" tool above.
Which route is easiest for senior pilgrims?
The helicopter route is the least physically demanding because it cuts out the long drives, which is why many senior pilgrims choose it. Acclimatisation is still needed, and the 3-day Parikrama over Dolma La is the same on every route — ponies and porters are available for those who need support.
Can I travel to Kailash independently?
No. Independent travel to Tibet is not permitted — every pilgrim must travel in an organised group with a licensed Tibetan guide, and the permits can only be obtained through a registered agency. Solo pilgrims are welcome to join our fixed-group departures.

Eligibility & Fitness

Who can join? Do I need to be Indian or win a lottery?
No lottery and no government selection. Anyone aged 12–70 with a valid passport can join — NRI / OCI, Indian, Nepali and all other nationalities. Solo travellers share a twin room with a same-gender pilgrim.
Is there an age limit?
Travellers aged 12 to 70 can apply for the Tibet permits. Children under 12 may travel only with special approval and family accompaniment. Pilgrims over 70 may be considered with detailed medical reports, a fitness certificate, a family member travelling with them and extra declarations — final approval rests with the Tibetan authorities.
How fit do I need to be? I have never trekked before.
The yatra is moderate to challenging mainly because of altitude, not technical difficulty, with several hours of walking a day on the Kora. Start 2–3 months before with regular cardio (walking, jogging or cycling), add leg and core strength work, and practise short hikes with a backpack. Get a doctor's clearance for high altitude.
Is it safe? What if I get altitude sickness?
Altitude sickness can affect anyone above about 3,000 m — watch for headache, dizziness, nausea, breathlessness or poor sleep. Our itinerary ascends gradually, every vehicle carries oxygen and a first-aid kit, and guides check on pilgrims daily. Mild symptoms need rest and water; severe ones mean immediate descent and medical care, with helicopter evacuation covered by your insurance if needed.
Who should not do the yatra?
It is not suitable during pregnancy, for people with severe heart, lung or neurological conditions, or for anyone who cannot walk independently. If you have heart disease, high blood pressure, asthma or COPD, severe diabetes, or recent major surgery, take detailed advice from your doctor first and tell us your full medical history.

Documents & Visa

Which visa and permits do I need?
Every pilgrim needs the China group visa (a separate document, not stamped in your passport, valid only for the approved dates and itinerary), the Tibet Travel Permit, the Alien's Travel Permit and military / foreign-affairs permits for the border areas. We arrange all of them. The visa fee is included for Indian passports; it is USD 260 for most foreign passports and USD 360 for US / Canada.
What documents do I need to send?
A passport valid for 6+ months beyond your return date with 2 blank pages and a colour scan of the photo page, passport photos on a white background, a doctor's fitness certificate, the booking form and your travel-insurance policy. NRI / OCI pilgrims also send a copy of the OCI card.
How long does the visa and permit process take?
Send your documents 45–60 days before departure so we can apply for the permits. The China group visa itself is processed in Kathmandu in about 3–5 working days once your documents are complete, so you hand over your original passport there. Please don't book non-refundable flights until we confirm your permit.
Can I apply for the visa or permits myself?
No. Individual applications are not accepted, and an individual Chinese tourist visa is not valid for Tibet travel. The group visa and all Tibet permits must be applied for through a licensed agency like us.

Spiritual & Practical

Can I take a holy dip in Lake Mansarovar?
Swimming in the open lake is restricted to protect it, so the holy bath (snan) is done the traditional way — lake water poured over you from buckets at the designated spots, under your guide's direction — followed by puja and hawan on the shore. Many pilgrims begin before dawn, in Brahma Muhurat.
Can I climb Mount Kailash or touch its base?
No. Mount Kailash (6,638 m) is sacred to four religions and has never been climbed — pilgrims walk the 52 km Parikrama around it instead. Charan Sparsh (touching the base) depends on the Chinese authorities; in 2025 no groups were permitted, so we do not promise it.
What is the weather like on the yatra?
Expect around 4–20 °C in the day and close to freezing at night, colder still at Dolma La (5,630 m). Bring warm layers, a waterproof jacket and broken-in trekking boots — we give every pilgrim a duffle bag, cap, jacket and trekking stick.
What food is served?
Pure vegetarian meals throughout — our own kitchen team cooks fresh Indian / Nepali food such as dal, rice, roti, sabzi and soup three times a day, even on the remote parts of the route.
Is there mobile network and Wi-Fi in Tibet?
Nepali SIM cards do not work in Tibet; a Chinese SIM can be bought in Kerung. Hotels in Kerung, Saga and Darchen have slow Wi-Fi, and there is little or no connection at Mansarovar and on the Kora — carry a power bank, as charging points are limited.
