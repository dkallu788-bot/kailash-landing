Kailash Mansarovar Overland Tour from Kathmandu

Join our Kailash Mansarovar Overland Tour from Kathmandu for a life-changing spiritual adventure. Witness the majestic beauty of Mount Kailash, take a holy dip in Lake Mansarovar, and explore the breathtaking Tibetan Plateau on a carefully organized pilgrimage.

Why Mount Kailash is sacred

Mount Kailash (6,638 m) is revered by four religions — Hinduism as the abode of Lord Shiva and Parvati, Buddhism as the home of Demchok, Jainism as Ashtapad where Rishabhadeva attained liberation, and the ancient Bon faith as the axis of the world. No human has ever climbed it; pilgrims instead walk the 52 km Parikrama (Kora), believed to cleanse the sins of a lifetime.

Nearby, Lake Mansarovar (4,558 m) — the highest freshwater lake in the world — was created in the mind of Lord Brahma. A holy dip and puja on its shores, especially under the Full Moon (Purnima), is considered one of the most auspicious acts a pilgrim can perform.

Day by Day

Detailed 13-Day Itinerary

Gradual ascent with a built-in acclimatization day. Distances and timings are approximate and may change with weather, road and Chinese border regulations.

DAY1

Arrive in Kathmandu (1,400 m)

Airport pickup 3-star hotel, Kathmandu Dinner

Our representative welcomes you at Tribhuvan International Airport and transfers you to the hotel. Evening yatra briefing — we collect original passports for the China group visa and hand over your Yatra kit (duffle bag, cap, jacket, trekking stick).

DAY2

Kathmandu Darshan — Pashupatinath & Boudhanath

UNESCO heritage sites Kathmandu B · L · D

Morning darshan at Pashupatinath Temple (one of the 12 Jyotirlingas' equivalent for Nepal), Boudhanath Stupa and Budhanilkantha (Sleeping Vishnu). Afternoon free for last-minute shopping. Visa processing continues.

DAY3

Drive Kathmandu → Rasuwagadhi border → Kerung (2,800 m)

~160 km · 7–8 hrs Nepal–China immigration Hotel, Kerung

Early departure along the Trishuli river and through Langtang National Park to Syabrubesi / Timure. Complete Nepal exit and Chinese immigration at Rasuwagadhi–Kerung, then a short 38 km drive to Kerung town. (If border timing requires, we overnight at Timure and cross next morning.)

DAY4

Acclimatization Day in Kerung

Rest & acclimatize Pakpa Monastery Hotel, Kerung

A crucial rest day for your body to adjust to altitude before the plateau. Gentle walk to the ancient Pakpa Monastery and Kerung market. Drink plenty of water, avoid exertion.

DAY5

Drive Kerung → Saga (4,450 m)

232 km · 6–7 hrs Labug La pass 5,050 m Hotel, Saga

Climb out of the Kerung valley onto the Tibetan Plateau, crossing Thong La and Labug La. Stunning views of Shishapangma and turquoise Peiku Tso lake before crossing the Brahmaputra (Yarlung Tsangpo) to reach Saga.

DAY6

Drive Saga → Lake Mansarovar (4,558 m)

~450 km · 8–9 hrs Mayum La pass Guesthouse, Mansarovar

A long but breathtaking drive across the plateau via Mayum La. Your first darshan of Mount Kailash appears on the horizon before you descend to the shores of Lake Mansarovar. Overnight in a lakeside guesthouse — on Full-Moon departures, tonight you watch Purnima rise over the holy lake.

DAY7

Mansarovar Puja, Holy Bath & Hawan → Darchen (4,660 m)

Holy dip & rituals Lake parikrama by bus + 35 km Hotel, Darchen

Sunrise holy bath in Lake Mansarovar, followed by puja and hawan on the shore. Vehicle parikrama of the lake with views of Rakshas Tal and Gurla Mandhata. Drive to Darchen, base of Mount Kailash. Arrange porters / ponies for the Kora.

DAY8

Kora Day 1 — Yam Dwar / Tarboche → Dirapuk (4,900 m)

12 km trek · 5–6 hrs Kailash north face Basic guesthouse, Dirapuk

Short drive to Yam Dwar (Tarboche), gateway of the Parikrama. Trek gently up the Lha Chu valley with Kailash's west face beside you. Dirapuk offers the closest, most dramatic view of the sheer north face of Kailash.

DAY9

Kora Day 2 — Dirapuk → Dolma La (5,630 m) → Zuthulpuk (4,790 m)

22 km trek · 8–9 hrs Highest point of the yatra Basic guesthouse, Zuthulpuk

The most demanding and most rewarding day. Pre-dawn start, steady climb to Dolma La Pass with prayer flags fluttering, then descend past the emerald Gauri Kund (Parvati's lake) into the Lham Chu valley to Zuthulpuk — the cave monastery of Milarepa.

DAY10

Kora Day 3 — Zuthulpuk → Darchen → Saga

8 km trek · 2–3 hrs ~470 km drive Hotel, Saga

Easy walk out of the valley to complete the sacred Parikrama. Our vehicle waits to take you back to Darchen for lunch, then the long drive back to Saga. Celebrate — you have completed the Kailash Kora.

DAY11

Drive Saga → Kerung

232 km · 6–7 hrs Hotel, Kerung B · L · D

Retrace the plateau route with a final look at Shishapangma and Peiku Tso, descending into the greener Kerung valley — warmer air and thicker oxygen at last.

DAY12

Drive Kerung → Border → Kathmandu

Immigration at Rasuwagadhi ~160 km · 7–8 hrs Hotel, Kathmandu

Exit China, re-enter Nepal and drive back to Kathmandu. Evening farewell dinner and certificate of Yatra completion.

DAY13

Departure from Kathmandu

Airport drop Breakfast

Transfer to the airport for your onward flight, or extend your trip with a Muktinath darshan, Everest mountain flight or Nepal tour. Om Namah Shivaya.

Preparation

Fitness, Altitude & Health

The yatra is rated moderate to challenging. No trekking experience is needed — but honest preparation makes the difference between enduring and enjoying it.

Get Fit — 2–3 months before

  • Walk 5–8 km daily, add stairs or inclines
  • Light cardio: jogging, cycling, swimming, yoga / pranayama
  • Ideal BMI 27 or below
  • Practise walking with a daypack and trekking stick
  • Stop smoking & alcohol at least 1 month before

Altitude Sickness (AMS)

Above 3,500 m anyone can get AMS regardless of fitness. Symptoms: headache, nausea, poor sleep, breathlessness, loss of appetite, dizziness.

  • Our itinerary climbs gradually with a rest day at Kerung
  • Drink 3–4 litres of water daily
  • Walk slowly, never over-exert; avoid sleeping pills & alcohol
  • Oxygen cylinders in every vehicle; guides monitor SpO₂
  • Diamox may be taken on doctor's advice

Medical Fitness

  • Doctor's fitness certificate for travel above 5,000 m required
  • Not recommended for severe heart disease, uncontrolled BP, asthma / severe respiratory illness, recent surgery, pregnancy
  • Carry all regular medicines for the full trip + 3 days
  • Do not bathe in cold high-altitude water other than the ritual dip
  • Ponies & porters available for those who need support on the Kora

Paperwork — We Handle It

Visa, Permits & Documents

Mount Kailash lies in the Tibet Autonomous Region of China. Independent travel is not permitted — every pilgrim must travel with a licensed operator who obtains the permits.

Permits we arrange for you

  • China Group Visa — issued in Kathmandu on a group paper; you must be in Kathmandu 2–3 working days before departure with your original passport
  • Tibet Travel Permit (TTP) — mandatory for all foreigners, arranged only through registered agencies
  • Alien's Travel Permit (ATP) — for restricted areas incl. Mansarovar & Kailash
  • Military Permit — for the western Tibet border region
  • Kailash & Mansarovar entry tickets — included
Timeline: Send documents ≥ 45–60 days before departure. Visa fees: included for Indian passports; USD 260 for most others, USD 360 for US / Canada. Citizens of visa-free countries still need the Tibet permits.

Not allowed through Chinese customs

  • Books, magazines or images about Tibet, the Dalai Lama or Tibetan politics
  • Drones, satellite phones, large religious literature in bulk
  • Photography at customs, army and police posts

Documents you send us

  • Passport valid 6+ months beyond return date, with 2 blank pages
  • Colour scan of passport bio page (all nationalities)
  • 4 passport-size photos, white background
  • Doctor's fitness certificate (high altitude, 5,000 m+)
  • Completed booking form & signed terms
  • Travel insurance policy copy
  • For OCI / NRI: OCI card copy or previous Indian passport if applicable
  • For minors: parental consent letter
Important: Do not book non-refundable international flights until we confirm your Tibet permit. Chinese authorities occasionally change rules at short notice — in 2025 all groups were denied Charan Sparsh (touching Kailash's base), so we no longer promise it rather than mislead pilgrims.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is booking open for Kailash Mansarovar Yatra 2027?
Yes. 2027 Full-Moon batches (May–September) are open for booking now. Seats are limited per batch by Chinese permit quotas, so we recommend reserving with a deposit as early as possible.
How much does the Kailash Overland Yatra cost?
USD 2,300 per person on twin-sharing basis for the 13-day fixed group departure from Kathmandu. This includes hotels, all vegetarian meals, transport, Tibet permits, visa assistance, guides, oxygen and the yatra kit. Not included: international flights, insurance, China visa fee for non-Indian passports, pony/porter and personal expenses. See the full list above.
Who is eligible? Do I need to be Indian or win a lottery?
No lottery, no government selection. Anyone aged 12–70 with a valid passport can join — Indian, NRI/OCI, Nepali, Australian, American, European and all other nationalities. Pilgrims over 70 may join with a doctor's certificate and our approval.
How fit do I need to be? I have never trekked before.
The yatra is rated moderate to challenging mainly because of altitude, not technical difficulty. Most pilgrims are ordinary devotees aged 40–65. Begin daily walking and light cardio 2–3 months before, aim for BMI 27 or below, and get a doctor's clearance. Ponies and porters are available on the Kora for those who need support.
What is the Kailash Parikrama and how hard is it?
The Parikrama (Kora) is the 52 km circumambulation of Mount Kailash — about 42 km on foot over 3 days plus 10 km by vehicle. Day 1 is a gentle 12 km; Day 2 is the tough 22 km crossing of Dolma La Pass at 5,630 m; Day 3 is an easy 8 km. It is believed to wash away the sins of a lifetime.
Do I need a Chinese visa? How are permits handled?
Yes — every pilgrim needs a China group visa plus the Tibet Travel Permit, Alien's Travel Permit and Military Permit. We arrange all of them; you simply send us your passport scan 45–60 days before departure and hand over your original passport in Kathmandu 2–3 working days before the trip. Visa fee is included for Indian passports, USD 260 for most others and USD 360 for US/Canada.
What documents do I need to send?
Passport valid 6+ months with 2 blank pages, colour passport scan, 4 passport photos, doctor's fitness certificate, completed booking form, and your travel insurance policy. OCI holders send the OCI card copy too.
Why are Full Moon departures special?
On Purnima night the reflection of the full moon on Lake Mansarovar is considered the most auspicious sight of the yatra — devotees believe gods descend to bathe in the lake at this time. We time these batches so you are at the lake on the full-moon night. They are the most in-demand dates and fill months in advance — at no extra cost.
What is the difference between the Government (MEA) Yatra and yours?
The MEA yatra goes via Lipulekh or Nathu La, is lottery-based, takes 22–25 days, and is open only to Indian citizens aged 18–70. Our overland yatra via Kathmandu is a direct confirmed booking, takes 13 days, runs May–September with Full-Moon options, and is open to all nationalities aged 12–70.
What is the accommodation and food like?
3-star hotels in Kathmandu; hotels with attached bathrooms in Kerung, Saga and Darchen; basic pilgrim guesthouses at Mansarovar and on the two Kora nights (shared rooms, no showers). Our own kitchen team cooks pure vegetarian Indian/Nepali meals — dal, rice, roti, sabzi, soup, tea — three times a day throughout.
Is it safe? What if I get altitude sickness?
Safety is our first priority. The itinerary ascends gradually with an acclimatization day at Kerung. Every vehicle carries oxygen cylinders and a first-aid kit; guides monitor pilgrims daily. If someone cannot continue, we arrange descent by vehicle or ambulance van and, in emergencies, helicopter evacuation covered by your insurance.
Can I climb Mount Kailash or touch its base (Charan Sparsh)?
Climbing Kailash is forbidden — it is sacred to four religions and has never been summited. Charan Sparsh (touching the base) is subject to Chinese authorities; in 2025 no groups were permitted, so we do not promise it. If it is allowed during your yatra, our guides will arrange it.
Can I travel solo? Can I bring children or elderly parents?
Solo pilgrims are welcome on fixed departures and share a twin room with another pilgrim of the same gender. Children from 12 years may join with a parent. Elderly pilgrims up to 70 (and beyond with medical clearance) join every year — many use ponies for the Kora.
What is your cancellation policy?
The booking deposit is non-refundable once permit processing has started. For cancellations 30+ days before departure the balance is refunded minus actual permit/visa costs; within 30 days charges apply as per our Terms & Conditions. No refund is possible for early return or unused services. We strongly recommend trip-cancellation insurance.
Is there mobile network and Wi-Fi in Tibet?
Nepali SIMs do not work in Tibet. Chinese SIM cards can be purchased in Kerung. Hotels in Kerung, Saga and Darchen have slow Wi-Fi; there is no connectivity at Mansarovar or during the Kora. Carry a power bank as charging points are limited.
How do I book?
1) WhatsApp us your preferred date and passport scan. 2) Pay the booking deposit. 3) Send the remaining documents; we process permits. 4) Pay the balance before departure or on arrival in Kathmandu. That's it — we take care of everything else.
